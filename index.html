The Architecture of Curiosity

How a fascination with how things work evolved into a passion for Computer Science.

My journey didn't start with code; it started with questions. Growing up in Vietnam, I was the kid who took apart toy cars not just to see the motor, but to understand the logic binding the components. That fundamental curiosity gradually evolved into an engineering mindset.

When I began writing programs, I felt that same amazement. A few lines of code could change what appeared on a screen. But over time, particularly after a failed drone test in my lab, I realized that behind every result is a structure. I wanted to understand that structure, not just use it. Writing code that works is different from building systems that remain reliable when things go wrong.

This mindset guided me across continents. Moving to the US and attending Life Prep Academy presented new challenges, like struggling initially with Physics. Instead of giving up, I learned to measure, analyze, and adapt my study habits—a resilience I now apply directly to my projects. Whether I'm analyzing flight data for my search-and-rescue drone idea or mapping out user flows for a new app, I focus on the process of learning.

I am applying to study Computer Science because I don't just want to make machines move—I want to learn how to build dependable systems that help communities in times of need.

To learn the basics of computer vision by attempting to control a computer cursor using only hand gestures captured by a webcam.

Process & Challenges

Implemented OpenCV and MediaPipe for hand tracking. I struggled significantly with lighting conditions and gesture recognition stability, which opened my eyes to the complexities of real-world computer vision.

Takeaway

A valuable introduction to Python libraries and the constraints of visual data processing.
